Electrical & HVAC Quantity Takeoffs

By Ryusei Kanzaki, XORA (operated by L.C. Co., Ltd.) ／ Updated: 2026-10-06

I prepare draft electrical and HVAC quantity takeoffs from plan drawings for small US electrical contractors, using AI to read and count, with every draft personally reviewed by me before it reaches you.

What is this?

I prepare draft electrical and HVAC quantity takeoffs from your plan drawings, using AI to read and count, with every draft personally reviewed by me, Ryusei Kanzaki, before it goes out. This is a draft to help you prepare bids faster. You remain responsible for final review, pricing, code compliance, and submission.

How much does it cost?

The free sample (one page) is for your first request only. Beyond that, pricing is $199 per project (small plans), paid through Upwork escrow if the project comes from Upwork, or invoiced directly otherwise - not hourly.

How fast is the turnaround?

I'll return the free sample within 48 hours for small plans. For larger plans, I'll tell you the turnaround after I've seen the drawing set.

What do I receive?

You get a marked-up PDF with every fixture numbered, plus an Excel sheet with live formulas, so every count traces back to the drawing. It also lists sheet references and assumptions/RFIs - the same format most estimators already work with.

What file formats do you accept?

PDF only, for now - scanned or photographed drawings aren't accepted right now (see "What I don't do" below). It does not need to be a current job - a past or redacted plan is fine for the free sample.

How do you count, and how accurate is it?

I use an AI tool (Anthropic’s Claude) to read the drawing and count, and I check the final counts myself. The free sample is there so you can judge accuracy and turnaround yourself before any money changes hands, instead of taking my word for it.

How is my drawing and data handled by AI?

I only use your drawings and my counts for this takeoff. I never pass them to a competing company or use them for any other job. 30 days after delivery (the free-fix period), I delete the working copies of your drawings and files. Ask me and I’ll delete them sooner. Email records are kept as business records. Model training is turned off for Claude. However, because I use a personal-tier plan, Anthropic may still store and use conversations that are flagged for safety review. If I miscounted, tell me within 30 days of delivery and I'll fix it free. New drawing revisions or added scope are a new request.

What I don't do (yet)

The following are not accepted right now.

  • Dense drawings (a high density of closely-spaced symbols): Not accepted right now. Counting by eye alone misses items on dense drawings, and a machine-assisted method for this density is still being tested and built out (the same size circle symbol can be reused for different fixture types, so counting shapes alone isn't enough either). See the case study below for how I am testing this.
  • Scanned or photographed drawings: PDF drawings with selectable text or vector shapes only, for now.
  • Large drawings / multi-building projects: Small plan sets only, for now.
  • Site visits, drawing creation or revision, construction: Outside the scope of this service.

Case study: testing the counting method on a public drawing

Not an actual client - this is a public bid drawing I used to test and improve my counting process. Source: County of Colusa, California - Administration Office electrical sheets (public bid documents; no drawing image is reproduced here).

Before: An early AI counting pass, reading cropped images of the first-floor lighting plan, found 35 fixtures. A separate AI count (two passes that agreed) found 81. The largest single cause of the gap was one fixture type (small recessed-LED symbols sitting on wiring lines), undercounted by 15 units; seven more fixture types weren't counted in the first pass at all.

After: Re-counting after the answer (81) was already known - machine-assisted shape detection for the round symbols, careful per-room counting for the rectangular ones - the AI reproduced 68 of the 81 units on its own; the shape-detection counts matched exactly. The remaining 13 units were copied from the earlier count rather than re-counted.

To test without knowing the answer, two separate AI counting passes counted a different part of the same drawing (the mezzanine level) without seeing each other's numbers: 11 vs. 12. One pass had read a single sideways-mounted strip fixture as part of a wiring run; the other had counted it. After checking its location, both agreed on 12.

This is why counting by eye alone is not something I rely on for dense drawings, and why dense drawings are not accepted as a paid service yet (see "What I don't do" above) - the method above is still being tested and built into the regular service.
